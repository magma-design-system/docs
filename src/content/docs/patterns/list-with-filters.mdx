The screen people open most in a management application: the list of the records they work on -
requests, documents, payments, files - where they find the ones they need. They search it,
narrow it down by status or by office, sort it and move through its pages, and they come back
to it after opening a record, expecting to find it as they left it.

<PatternPreview id="list-with-filters" />

## When to use it

Use it when people look for records in a set too large to read through: dozens of records or
more, with a few properties worth filtering on. Something else fits better:

- With a handful of records, show them all: filters and pages only get in the way.
- When the records are visual, such as images or documents to preview, show them as cards in a
  grid; the search, the filters and the pages stay the same.
- When people pick one record to go on with a task, such as a recipient in a form, a search
  field with suggestions is quicker than a whole list.

## Anatomy

1. **Title and description**: what the list holds, as the heading of the page.
2. **Search and filters**: a form with the role of a search, with a field that searches the
   number and the subject, a select for each property with a few values, and the order.
3. **Status filter**: the statuses as an `mds-filter`, one at a time, each with the number of
   records it would show.
4. **Summary**: which records are on screen out of how many, and the button that clears the
   filters when there are any.
5. **Results**: one row per record, its number linking to its page.
6. **Pages**: the paginator, only when there is more than one page.

Three states replace or join the results: **loading** (a spinner in the search field, while
the results already there stay), **no results** (a message with a way out), **error** (a
banner with a way to try again).

## Behavior

- **The back end does the work.** It searches, counts, sorts and pages the records, and the
  page receives one page of results. Filtering in the browser works only while the whole set
  fits in the page, and sets grow.
- **The query is in the address.** Search, status, office, order and page are parameters of the
  address, so a reload, a bookmark or a link sent to a colleague shows the same results, and
  Back from a record returns to the list as it was. Values left to their default stay out, to
  keep the address short.
- **A new filter starts from the first page.** Any change but the page goes back to page 1:
  page 4 of a smaller set may not exist. An address that asks for a page past the end gets the
  last one.
- **One query at a time.** The search waits for a pause in the typing (300 ms) instead of
  asking at every key, and a new query cancels the one still running, so an older, slower
  answer never replaces a newer one.
- **The results stay while the next ones load.** The spinner in the search field, and
  `aria-busy` on the table, tell that new results are coming; the page does not flash empty.
- **Counts follow the other filters.** Each status shows how many records it would show with
  the search and the office chosen, so an empty status is visible before it is picked.
- **No results is not an error.** When nothing matches, the page says so and offers to clear
  the filters. When the request fails, it says the results could not be loaded and offers to
  try again, instead of showing an empty list.

## Accessibility

- The filters are a form with `role="search"`, a landmark screen reader users can jump to.
  Every control has a visible label, given by `mds-input-field`: the placeholder is an example,
  not the label.
- The summary has `role="status"`: after each change, screen readers read out which records
  are shown out of how many, while the focus stays on the control just used, so people can go
  on refining.
- The status of each record is the text of its badge, not only its color.
- The number of each record is the link to its page, and the name of the link. A button that
  says "Open" on every row would sound the same on every row.
- The table keeps its column headers, and on a narrow screen it scrolls sideways instead of
  squeezing the columns: its cells stay on one line.

## Content

- Name the list after what it holds, in the plural: "Requests", not "Request list" or
  "Search".
- The label of the search field says what it is; the placeholder says what it searches:
  "Number or subject".
- Name the statuses as the page of a record names them, and list them in the order of the
  process.
- Count the results, and use the singular for one: "1 request", "11-20 of 46 requests".
- When nothing matches, say so and say how to get out of it: "No requests match", with
  "Clear filters" - not "No data".
- Write dates in the format of the language of the page.
