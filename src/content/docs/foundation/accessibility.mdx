In Italy, the software of the public administration has to be accessible by law: Law 4/2004
and the AgID guidelines require it to meet EN 301 549, the European standard that includes
WCAG. Since June 2025 the European Accessibility Act extends similar requirements to many
products and services sold to the public. For Magma it comes before the law: it is the first
of its [principles](../principles/).

## The target

Magma is designed for WCAG 2.x, level AA. It is a design target, not a certification: the
stories of every component run automated accessibility checks, and there is no independent
audit yet. A product built with Magma is accessible when it is tested as a whole, not
because its parts are.

## Who does what

The components take care of what is the same everywhere: the roles and states that assistive
technologies read, the keyboard, the visible focus, color pairs measured for contrast, and
their own built-in texts in Italian, English, Spanish and Greek.

The product takes care of what only it knows: the order of the headings, the labels of fields
and of buttons with only an icon, the alternative text of its images, the order of the focus
across the page, the error messages, the flow of each task.

## Contrast

Text and icons that carry meaning meet the AA contrast ratios. Paint them with the color
roles, in the [pairs they are measured in](../../tokens/color/#pairing-rules): any other pair
is not measured. Users who need more contrast ask for it with the contrast preference, and
the roles [follow it](../../tokens/themes-and-preferences/).

## Never color alone

A state, a category or a choice is never told by color only: it also has a text, an icon or a
shape. Many people do not tell red from green, and a screen reader does not read colors.

## Keyboard

- Everything that works with a pointer works with the keyboard, in an order that follows the
  reading of the page.
- The focus is always visible.
- Standard controls answer to standard keys: Enter and Space press a button, Esc closes a
  dialog, the arrows move within a group. A new kind of interaction needs a standard
  alternative, and instructions.

## The focus follows the user

The focus moves only when the user does something that asks for it. Moving it otherwise
makes people lose their place, and screen reader users lose it without seeing where it went.

<Usages>
<mds-usage variant="do">

Opening a dialog moves the focus into it; closing it brings the focus back to the button that
opened it.

</mds-usage>
<mds-usage variant="dont">

A notification that arrives while the user is typing takes the focus.

</mds-usage>
<mds-usage variant="do">

Submitting a form with errors moves the focus to the first of them.

</mds-usage>
<mds-usage variant="dont">

An autosave moves the focus to its confirmation message.

</mds-usage>
</Usages>

The only exception is a task that cannot go on: then the product stops the user, and says
why.

## Preferences

Users set their preferences in the system or in the product, with `mds-pref`: light or dark
mode, more contrast, less motion, lower energy consumption. The components follow them, and
so does the code of the product when it paints with the tokens. Nothing essential happens
only through motion: with less motion, transitions get shorter or stop, and the content stays
the same.

## Text and zoom

- Pages work at 200% zoom and with larger text: no fixed heights on text, no text inside
  images.
- An image that carries information says it in its alternative text; a decorative image has
  an empty one.
- Every page declares its language, so screen readers pronounce it correctly and the
  components pick their own texts.

## Testing

Automated checks find only part of the problems. Before a release, go through the main tasks:

- with the keyboard only;
- with a screen reader: NVDA or JAWS on Windows, VoiceOver on macOS and iOS, TalkBack on
  Android;
- at 200% zoom, in dark mode and with more contrast.
