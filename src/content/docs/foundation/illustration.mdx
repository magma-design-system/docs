An illustration costs attention, and weight on the page: it has to give something back. In
Maggioli's products it explains, welcomes or marks a moment; it does not decorate the screens
where people work.

Magma has no library of interface illustrations yet: the artworks of the group, its logos
and the marks of its products, are in [Brand assets](../../brand/). Until there is one,
these decisions guide whoever draws or chooses an illustration for a product.

## When to use one

- To explain what words alone make hard: how a procedure goes, how a service works.
- To fill a space that would otherwise be empty, and say what to do next.
- To welcome: the first access, a guided tour, a new feature.

Not on the screens where people work, such as lists, forms and tables: there, the space
belongs to the data. And not to soften an error: a serious problem needs a clear message,
not a drawing.

## Where they go

- **Empty states**, with `mds-zero`: the illustration, a short message and one action.
- **Guided tours and announcements**: one illustration per step or per news item, all from
  the same family.
- **Editorial pages**: the pages that present a product or a publication, where the brand
  steps forward.

## How they are drawn

- **One idea each.** If an illustration needs a caption to be understood, it says too much.
- **One family per product.** The same line, palette and perspective in every illustration
  of a product: a mix of styles makes a product look patched together.
- **Few colors, from Magma's palettes**, less saturated than the actions, so that an
  illustration never competes with a button.
- **No text inside.** Text in an image cannot be translated, read by a screen reader or
  enlarged: it goes in the page, next to the image.
- **Light.** SVG whenever possible, and nothing a product cannot do without when the user asks
  for lower consumption.
- **Both modes.** No white background baked in: an illustration is checked in dark mode too.
- **People as they are.** When an illustration shows people, it shows the variety of those
  who use the products: ages, genders, origins, disabilities.

## Accessibility

- Most illustrations are decorative: their alternative text is empty, and the message is in
  the text next to them.
- An illustration that carries information says it in its alternative text.
- An animated illustration stops when the user asks for less motion, and never loops without
  a way to stop it.
