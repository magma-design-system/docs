Words can shut people out as surely as a button the keyboard cannot reach. Maggioli's
products speak to everyone who works in and with the public administration, so they do not
assume who that person is: their gender, their abilities, their origin, their age.

## In general

- **The person comes first**, not their condition: a person with a disability, not a
  disabled.
- **Say what you mean.** Metaphors drawn from disability, gender or origin say it badly, and
  they do not translate.
- **Ask only what is needed.** Gender, or any other personal data, only when a procedure
  requires it, with the words and the values that procedure uses, and saying why.
- **Accept people as they are.** A form takes the names people have: apostrophes, accents,
  spaces, more than one surname (D'Angelo, Nicol&ograve;, De Santis Rossi), and the documents and
  addresses of those who are not Italian.

## Disability

Since 2024, Italian law itself speaks this way: Legislative Decree 62/2024 replaced
"handicap" with "condition of disability", and "disabled" and "differently abled" with
"person with a disability", in every law.

- "People with disabilities", not "the disabled" or "the handicapped".
- "Has" or "lives with", not "suffers from" or "is afflicted by".
- "Uses a wheelchair", not "confined to a wheelchair".
- "Most users" or "users without disabilities", not "normal users".
- Say what you mean, not "blind to", "deaf to", "crazy" or "paralyzed": "unaware of",
  "ignores", "surprising", "stopped".

In the text of the interface, prefer on and off, active and inactive, to enabled and
disabled: `disabled` is the name of an attribute in the code, not a word for users.

## Gender

- Address the user directly, as "you".
- When the gender of a person is not known, write about the role or use "they".
- Do not tie roles to a gender: the mayor, the judge and the clerk can be anyone.

### In Italian

Italian marks gender in nouns, adjectives and participles, so it needs more care:

- **The generic masculine leaves out half the readers.** Prefer collective and neutral forms:
  "le persone", "il personale", "la cittadinanza", "chi presenta la domanda".
- **Rewrite rather than mark.** "Sei sicuro di voler uscire?" becomes "Vuoi uscire?";
  "Benvenuto" becomes "Ti diamo il benvenuto".
- **Feminine titles for women**: "la sindaca", "l'avvocata", "l'assessora", or the title the
  person uses for herself.
- **No asterisks, no schwa.** "Tutt\*" and "tutt&#601;" are read badly or skipped by screen
  readers, and many people do not know how to read them: rewrite the sentence instead.
- **Double forms sparingly.** "Cittadine e cittadini" works in a title or a greeting, not in
  every sentence of a text.

## Origin and culture

- No colors as metaphors of good and bad: a blocklist and an allowlist, not a blacklist and a
  whitelist.
- No master and slave for systems: primary and replica. The main branch of the Magma
  repository is called main, not master, for the same reason.
- What is unfamiliar to the writer is not exotic or strange to the reader.

## Age

Do not assume that older people struggle with technology, or that young people do not: "so
easy even your grandmother can use it" offends grandmothers, and helps nobody.
