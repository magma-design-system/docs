`@maggioli-design-system/design-tokens` publishes the colors of Magma for iOS as Swift
files, generated with every release from the same sources as the stylesheets. The files
below come from the version this site is built with: an app needs neither npm nor the
package to use them.

## Files

<PlatformFiles platform="ios" />

The two files hold the same colors as `UIColor` constants (UIKit) and differ in their names
only: add one of them to your target. `StyleDictionary.swift` keeps the group in each name,
`StyleDictionary.colorSurfaceNeutralLightDefault`; `StyleDictionaryColor.swift` drops it,
`StyleDictionaryColor.surfaceNeutralLightDefault`.

The package also has an Objective-C version in `dist/ios/`, which does not compile in this
version: the enumeration of its colors has no name.

## Names

A color is named by its group, family, mode and step, in camel case:
`surfaceNeutralLightDefault`, `toneNeutralDark10`.

- **Palettes**: `tone`, `label`, `status`, `brand` and `variant`, with the steps `1` to `10`
  of [Color](../color/). A tone adds `Seed`, the paper of its mode; the other families add
  `Color`, their own color: `statusErrorLightColor`.
- **Roles of a family**: `surface` (`Sunken`, `Muted`, `Default`, `Raised`, `Overlay`),
  `border` (`Muted`, `Default`, `Strong`) and `text` (`Default`, `Muted`, `Subtle`,
  `Disabled`), for the families `Neutral`, `Porcelain`, `Bisque`, `Info`, `Success`,
  `Warning` and `Error`.
- **Mode**: `Light` or `Dark`. Both modes are in the same file.

## Dark mode

Each mode is a constant of its own. To follow the system, make a dynamic color of the two:

```swift
let surfaceDefault = UIColor { traits in
  traits.userInterfaceStyle == .dark
    ? StyleDictionaryColor.surfaceNeutralDarkDefault
    : StyleDictionaryColor.surfaceNeutralLightDefault
}
```

In SwiftUI, wrap it: `Color(uiColor: surfaceDefault)`.

## Roles and themes

On the web, the surface, border and text roles of a page read one family, chosen by the
named theme: `neutral` by default, `porcelain` for `cool`, `bisque` for `warm`. Here the
choice is the app's: the `Neutral` colors are those of the default theme. The other
semantic roles are not in the files: [Color](../color/) lists the step each one points at,
in light and in dark mode.

## Not in the files yet

iOS gets the colors only: no type styles, spacing, radii, shadows or motion, and none of
what the higher contrast preference changes on the web.
