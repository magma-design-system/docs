A page is made of sections, and many of them come back from one product to the next. A building
block is one of those sections, made of Magma components and always arranged the same way: the
header of a page with its title and its actions, a bar of filters, the summary of the results, a
section of a form, a bar of actions.

A building block holds no data and no business logic. Its rules are about structure and layout:
which parts it has and which of them are optional, in which order they come, how it adapts to
the width of the screen. What happens when people use it belongs to the
[patterns](../patterns/).

## Components, blocks, patterns

- A **component** is one Magma element, such as a button or a field: Magma ships it ready, and
  you import it.
- A **building block** puts components together into a section of a page: it is about space.
- A **pattern** is the solution to a task that people carry out again and again: it is about
  time - what follows an action, where the state lives, loading, errors, focus. It uses
  components and building blocks, and you apply it rather than import it.

## Where they come from

The building blocks are not designed in advance: they come out of the patterns. A section of a
page becomes a block when it comes back in two or three patterns, so that it is written once and
every page that needs it builds it the same way.

Each block will have the pages a component has: an overview with how to apply it with an AI
coding agent, the block itself, its parts and the components it is made of, then its
guidelines - the rules that always hold, how it adapts to the width - its code and the mistakes
to avoid. The first one being written is the page header, which comes back in every pattern
written so far.
