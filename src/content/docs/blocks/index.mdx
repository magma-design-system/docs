Between a component and a whole screen there is a middle size. A building block is a group of
Magma components that screens use again and again, in the same arrangement: the header of a
page with its title and its actions, a bar of filters, the summary of the results, a section of
a form, a bar of actions, an empty or an error state, a dialog that asks for confirmation.

A building block holds no data and no business logic. Its rules are about structure and layout:
which parts it has and which of them are optional, in which order they come, how it adapts to
the width of the screen.

## Components, blocks, patterns

- A **component** is one Magma element, such as a button or a field: Magma ships it ready.
- A **building block** puts components together into a part of a screen.
- A **pattern** is a whole screen or task: building blocks, plus what happens over time -
  where the state lives, what follows an action, loading, errors, focus.

## Where they come from

The building blocks are not designed in advance: they come out of the [patterns](../patterns/).
A part of a screen becomes a block when it comes back in two or three patterns, so that it is
written once and every screen that needs it builds it the same way.

Each block will have its page: its parts, the rules that always hold, its states, the components
it is made of and its code.
